#+title: Dictionaries

Small inputs, such as individual records, compress poorly on their own: there
is too little in each for patterns to repeat. A dictionary primes the compressor
with what a kind of data has in common, so each record can be compressed, and
later decompressed, on its own while still compressing well.

[[@ref][~train_dictionary~]] makes a dictionary from samples of the data, and
[[@ref][~ZstdDictionary~]] loads one saved earlier, or made by =zstd --train=.

#+begin_src julia
dictionary = train_dictionary(sample_records; size = 1 << 20)
write("records.dict", dictionary)
dictionary = ZstdDictionary(read("records.dict"))
#+end_src

Pass a dictionary to a [[@ref][~ZstdCompressor~]] or [[@ref][~ZstdWriter~]] to compress with it.
Every frame records the ID of the dictionary it needs, which [[@ref][~ZstdLib.dictionaryid~]]
reads, so a [[@ref][~ZstdDecompressor~]] or [[@ref][~ZstdReader~]] given several dictionaries
decompresses each frame with the right one. Changing to a newly trained
dictionary thus leaves older data readable, so long as the old dictionary is
kept too.

Any other bytes, such as an earlier version of a document, also make a
dictionary, used as raw content. Frames compressed with raw content name no
dictionary, as do those made by =zstd --no-dictID=. A decompressor gives such
frames the raw content among its dictionaries, or else its only dictionary,
like =zstd -D=.

#+begin_src @docs
ZstdDictionary
train_dictionary
ZstdLib.dictionaryid
#+end_src

* Choosing a size

A dictionary can hold only what its samples have in common, and training wants
around a hundred times its size in samples. A larger dictionary can help
records with much shared vocabulary, but past a point gains nothing, and may
compress worse, so compare a few sizes by compressing held-out records.
